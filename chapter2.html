Chapter 2: Kinematics

Complete Physics Notes in Simple English

1. Introduction to Kinematics

Kinematics is the branch of physics that deals with the motion of objects without discussing the forces responsible for that motion.

Examples:

  • A car moving on a road.
  • A ball falling from a height.
  • A train moving on a railway track.
  • A person walking in a street.

2. Rest and Motion

Rest

An object is said to be at rest if its position does not change with respect to its surroundings or a reference point.

Example: A book lying on a table is at rest with respect to the table.

Motion

An object is said to be in motion if its position changes with respect to its surroundings or a reference point with time.

Example: A moving car is in motion with respect to the road.

Reference Point

A fixed point used to describe the position or motion of an object is called a reference point.

3. Types of Motion

  • Linear Motion: Motion along a straight line. Example: A car moving on a straight road.
  • Circular Motion: Motion along a circular path. Example: The hands of a clock.
  • Rotatory Motion: Motion of an object around its own axis. Example: A spinning fan.
  • Vibratory Motion: Repeated to-and-fro motion about a mean position. Example: A vibrating tuning fork.

4. Distance and Displacement

Distance

Distance is the total length of the actual path travelled by an object.

  • It is a scalar quantity.
  • It has magnitude only.
  • Its SI unit is metre (m).

Displacement

Displacement is the shortest straight-line change in position from the initial point to the final point, with direction.

  • It is a vector quantity.
  • It has magnitude and direction.
  • Its SI unit is metre (m).
Example: A student walks 5 metres east and then 3 metres west.

Distance = 5 + 3 = 8 m
Displacement = 5 - 3 = 2 m east

5. Scalar and Vector Quantities

Scalar Quantity

A physical quantity that has magnitude only and no direction is called a scalar quantity.

Examples: Distance, speed, mass, time and temperature.

Vector Quantity

A physical quantity that has both magnitude and direction is called a vector quantity.

Examples: Displacement, velocity, acceleration and force.

6. Speed

Speed is the distance travelled by an object in unit time.

Speed = Distance / Time

SI unit of speed is metre per second (m/s).

Solved Numerical:
A car travels 100 metres in 20 seconds. Find its speed.

Given:
Distance = 100 m
Time = 20 s

Formula: Speed = Distance / Time
Speed = 100 / 20
Answer = 5 m/s

7. Types of Speed

Uniform Speed

An object has uniform speed if it covers equal distances in equal intervals of time.

Variable Speed

An object has variable speed if it covers unequal distances in equal intervals of time.

Average Speed

Average speed is the total distance travelled divided by the total time taken.

Average Speed = Total Distance / Total Time

8. Velocity

Velocity is the displacement covered by an object in unit time in a particular direction.

Velocity = Displacement / Time

SI unit of velocity is metre per second (m/s).

Velocity is a vector quantity because it has both magnitude and direction.

Example: A person moves 20 metres east in 5 seconds.
Velocity = 20 / 5
Answer = 4 m/s east

9. Types of Velocity

  • Uniform Velocity: Equal displacements in equal intervals of time in the same direction.
  • Variable Velocity: Velocity changes in magnitude, direction, or both.
  • Average Velocity: Total displacement divided by total time.
Average Velocity = Total Displacement / Total Time

10. Acceleration

Acceleration is the rate of change of velocity with respect to time.

Acceleration = (Final Velocity - Initial Velocity) / Time
a = (v - u) / t

Where:

  • a = Acceleration
  • v = Final velocity
  • u = Initial velocity
  • t = Time

SI unit of acceleration is metre per second squared (m/s²).

Solved Numerical:
A car increases its velocity from 10 m/s to 30 m/s in 5 seconds. Find acceleration.

u = 10 m/s
v = 30 m/s
t = 5 s

a = (v - u) / t
a = (30 - 10) / 5
Answer = 4 m/s²

11. Types of Acceleration

Uniform Acceleration

When velocity changes by equal amounts in equal intervals of time, acceleration is called uniform acceleration.

Variable Acceleration

When velocity changes by unequal amounts in equal intervals of time, acceleration is called variable acceleration.

Positive Acceleration

When acceleration is in the positive direction of the chosen coordinate system, it is called positive acceleration.

Negative Acceleration

When acceleration is in the negative direction of the chosen coordinate system, it is called negative acceleration.

Negative acceleration does not always mean that an object is slowing down. It depends on the direction of velocity.

12. Graphs of Motion

Distance-Time Graph

A distance-time graph shows how distance changes with time.

  • A straight sloping line shows constant speed.
  • A horizontal line shows that the object is at rest.
  • A curved line shows changing speed.

Velocity-Time Graph

A velocity-time graph shows how velocity changes with time.

  • A horizontal line shows constant velocity.
  • A sloping line shows acceleration.
  • The slope represents acceleration.
  • The area under the graph represents displacement.

13. Equations of Uniformly Accelerated Motion

These equations are used when acceleration is constant.

First Equation: v = u + at
Second Equation: s = ut + ½at²
Third Equation: v² = u² + 2as

Where:

  • u = Initial velocity
  • v = Final velocity
  • a = Constant acceleration
  • t = Time
  • s = Displacement

14. Solved Numerical Using Equations of Motion

Question: A car starts from rest and accelerates at 2 m/s² for 5 seconds. Find its final velocity.

Given:

  • u = 0 m/s
  • a = 2 m/s²
  • t = 5 s

Formula: v = u + at

v = 0 + (2 × 5)

Final velocity = 10 m/s

15. Free Fall

When an object falls towards the Earth under the effect of gravity alone, its motion is called free fall.

Acceleration due to gravity near the Earth's surface is approximately:

g = 9.8 m/s²

For an object falling freely from rest, air resistance is ignored.

17. Important MCQs

1. Kinematics is the study of:

A) Heat   B) Motion   C) Light   D) Sound

Answer: B) Motion

2. SI unit of speed is:

A) m   B) s   C) m/s   D) kg

Answer: C) m/s

3. Which quantity is a vector?

A) Distance   B) Speed   C) Mass   D) Velocity

Answer: D) Velocity

4. SI unit of acceleration is:

A) m/s   B) m/s²   C) kg   D) N

Answer: B) m/s²

5. Distance divided by time gives:

A) Force   B) Speed   C) Acceleration   D) Mass

Answer: B) Speed

6. The slope of a velocity-time graph represents:

A) Distance   B) Speed   C) Acceleration   D) Mass

Answer: C) Acceleration

7. The area under a velocity-time graph represents:

A) Displacement   B) Acceleration   C) Force   D) Mass

Answer: A) Displacement

8. Approximate acceleration due to gravity is:

A) 5.8 m/s²   B) 9.8 m/s²   C) 15 m/s²   D) 20 m/s²

Answer: B) 9.8 m/s²

18. Quick Revision

  • Kinematics deals with motion without studying its causes.
  • Distance is a scalar quantity.
  • Displacement is a vector quantity.
  • Speed = Distance / Time.
  • Velocity = Displacement / Time.
  • Acceleration = Change in velocity / Time.
  • SI unit of speed and velocity is m/s.
  • SI unit of acceleration is m/s².
  • Slope of velocity-time graph gives acceleration.
  • Area under velocity-time graph gives displacement.
