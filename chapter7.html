Chapter 7: Properties of Matter

Easy Physics Notes | GM NOTES

1. Matter

Matter is anything that has mass and occupies space.

Examples:

  • Water
  • Air
  • Wood
  • Iron
  • Milk

2. States of Matter

Matter commonly exists in three basic states:

  • Solid: Has a fixed shape and fixed volume.
  • Liquid: Has a fixed volume but no fixed shape.
  • Gas: Has no fixed shape and no fixed volume.

Examples:

StateExamplesShapeVolume
SolidIce, wood, stoneFixedFixed
LiquidWater, oil, milkNot fixedFixed

3. Properties of Matter

Properties of matter are the characteristics that help us describe and identify matter.

Important Properties:

  • Mass: The amount of matter in an object.
  • Volume: The space occupied by an object.
  • Density: Mass per unit volume.
  • Shape: The external form of an object.
  • Compressibility: The ability of matter to decrease in volume under pressure.

4. Molecular Forces

Molecular forces are the forces of attraction between the molecules of matter.

Types:

  • Cohesive Force: The force of attraction between molecules of the same substance.
  • Adhesive Force: The force of attraction between molecules of different substances.

Molecular forces are generally strongest in solids and weakest in gases.

5. Solid

A solid is a state of matter that has a fixed shape and fixed volume.

Properties:

  • Molecules are closely packed.
  • Strong attractive forces act between molecules.
  • Molecules vibrate around fixed positions.
  • Solids are difficult to compress.
  • Solids have a definite shape and volume.

Examples:

Ice, wood, iron and stone.

6. Liquid

A liquid is a state of matter that has a fixed volume but no fixed shape.

Properties:

  • Molecules are close to each other.
  • Molecules can move and slide past one another.
  • Liquids can flow.
  • Liquids take the shape of their container.
  • Liquids are difficult to compress.

Examples:

Water, oil and milk.

7. Gas

A gas is a state of matter that has no fixed shape and no fixed volume.

Properties:

  • Molecules are far apart.
  • Attractive forces are very weak.
  • Molecules move freely and randomly.
  • Gases fill the complete container.
  • Gases can be compressed easily.

Examples:

Oxygen, nitrogen, carbon dioxide and air.

8. Change of States of Matter

Matter can change from one state to another by heating or cooling.

1. Melting

Melting is the change of a solid into a liquid by heating.

Example: Ice changes into water.

2. Freezing

Freezing is the change of a liquid into a solid by cooling.

Example: Water changes into ice.

3. Boiling

Boiling is the process in which a liquid changes into gas throughout the liquid at its boiling point.

Example: Water changes into steam.

4. Condensation

Condensation is the change of a gas into a liquid by cooling.

Example: Water droplets form on a cold glass.

5. Evaporation

Evaporation is the process in which a liquid changes into gas from its surface. It can occur below the boiling point.

Example: Wet clothes dry in sunlight.

10. Density

Density is the mass per unit volume of a substance.

Density = Mass / Volume
ρ = m / V

Where:

  • ρ = Density
  • m = Mass
  • V = Volume

SI unit of density is kg/m³.

Example:

A body has a mass of 20 kg and a volume of 4 m³. Find its density.

ρ = m / V
ρ = 20 / 4
ρ = 5 kg/m³

Answer: Density is 5 kg/m³.

11. Kinetic Molecular Model of Matter

The kinetic molecular model explains the behavior of matter by considering the motion of its molecules.

Main Points:

  • All matter is made up of tiny particles called molecules or atoms.
  • These particles are always in motion.
  • There are spaces between the particles.
  • Attractive forces exist between particles.
  • The average kinetic energy of particles increases with temperature.

12. Brownian Motion

Brownian motion is the continuous random movement of tiny particles suspended in a liquid or gas.

Example:

Pollen grains suspended in water move randomly because they are hit by water molecules.

Importance:

  • It provides evidence that molecules are in constant motion.
  • It supports the kinetic molecular theory of matter.

13. Forces and Kinetic Theory

The kinetic theory explains matter by describing the motion of its particles and the forces between them.

  • In solids, attractive forces are strong and particles vibrate around fixed positions.
  • In liquids, particles remain close but can move past one another.
  • In gases, particles are far apart and move freely.
  • Temperature is related to the average kinetic energy of particles.

14. Gases and Kinetic Theory

According to the kinetic theory, a gas consists of tiny particles that move continuously and randomly.

Main Points:

  • Gas particles are far apart.
  • They move in random directions.
  • They collide with each other and the walls of the container.
  • Collisions are considered elastic in the ideal gas model.
  • Gas pressure is caused by collisions of particles with the container walls.
  • Increasing temperature increases the average kinetic energy of gas particles.

15. Temperature and Kinetic Energy

Temperature is a measure of how hot or cold a body is. In the kinetic model, the Kelvin temperature of a gas is proportional to the average translational kinetic energy of its particles.

For an ideal gas:

Average Kinetic Energy = 3/2 kT
  • k = Boltzmann constant
  • T = Absolute temperature in kelvin

When temperature increases, the average kinetic energy of gas particles also increases.

16. Pressure of a Gas

Gas pressure is the force exerted by gas particles per unit area on the walls of their container.

Pressure = Force / Area
P = F / A

SI unit of pressure is Pascal (Pa).

Gas pressure is produced by collisions of gas particles with the walls of the container.

17. Pressure-Volume Relationship in Gases

For a fixed amount of gas at constant temperature, pressure increases when volume decreases.

Similarly, pressure decreases when volume increases.

This is an inverse relationship between pressure and volume.

P ∝ 1/V

18. Boyle's Law

Boyle's law states that the pressure of a fixed mass of gas is inversely proportional to its volume, provided the temperature remains constant.

P ∝ 1/V
PV = Constant
P₁V₁ = P₂V₂

Where:

  • P₁ = Initial pressure
  • V₁ = Initial volume
  • P₂ = Final pressure
  • V₂ = Final volume

Important Conditions:

  • The amount of gas remains constant.
  • Temperature remains constant.
  • Pressure and volume change inversely.

Example:

A gas has an initial pressure of 2 Pa and volume of 6 m³. If its volume becomes 3 m³ at constant temperature, find the final pressure.

Given:

  • P₁ = 2 Pa
  • V₁ = 6 m³
  • V₂ = 3 m³

Formula:

P₁V₁ = P₂V₂

Solution:

2 × 6 = P₂ × 3
12 = 3P₂
P₂ = 12 / 3
P₂ = 4 Pa

Answer: Final pressure is 4 Pa.

19. Applications of Boyle's Law

1. Breathing

When the volume of the chest cavity increases, pressure inside the lungs decreases and air enters the lungs.

2. Syringe

When the piston is pulled, the volume inside the syringe increases and pressure decreases. Liquid can then enter through the needle.

3. Bicycle Pump

When the piston is pushed, the air volume decreases and its pressure increases, helping air enter the tyre.

4. Air Compression

Compressing air into a smaller volume increases its pressure when temperature is kept constant.

21. Important MCQs

1. Matter is anything that has:

A) Only mass   B) Only volume   C) Mass and volume   D) Only shape

Answer: C) Mass and volume

2. Which state has a fixed shape and volume?

A) Gas   B) Liquid   C) Solid   D) Steam

Answer: C) Solid

3. Which state has a fixed volume but no fixed shape?

A) Solid   B) Liquid   C) Gas   D) Air

Answer: B) Liquid

4. The change of solid into liquid is called:

A) Freezing   B) Melting   C) Condensation   D) Evaporation

Answer: B) Melting

5. The change of liquid into gas is called:

A) Freezing   B) Melting   C) Evaporation   D) Condensation

Answer: C) Evaporation

6. The SI unit of density is:

A) kg   B) m³   C) kg/m³   D) N

Answer: C) kg/m³

7. Brownian motion is:

A) Regular motion   B) Random motion   C) Circular motion   D) Uniform motion

Answer: B) Random motion

8. Which state can be compressed easily?

A) Solid   B) Liquid   C) Gas   D) Ice

Answer: C) Gas

9. Boyle's law is valid at constant:

A) Pressure   B) Temperature   C) Volume   D) Mass only

Answer: B) Temperature

10. According to Boyle's law, pressure and volume are:

A) Directly proportional   B) Inversely proportional   C) Equal   D) Unrelated

Answer: B) Inversely proportional

11. Gas pressure is caused by:

A) Gravity only   B) Particle collisions with container walls   C) Color   D) Shape

Answer: B) Particle collisions with container walls

12. The SI unit of pressure is:

A) Joule   B) Newton   C) Pascal   D) Watt

Answer: C) Pascal
