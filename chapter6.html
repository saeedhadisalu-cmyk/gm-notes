Chapter 6: Gravitation

Easy Physics Notes | GM NOTES

1. Gravitation

Gravitation is the force of attraction between any two objects having mass.

Examples:

  • The Earth attracts objects towards its center.
  • The Moon revolves around the Earth due to gravity.
  • The planets revolve around the Sun due to gravitational force.

2. Newton's Law of Gravitation

Newton's law of universal gravitation states that every object in the universe attracts every other object with a force.

This force is directly proportional to the product of their masses and inversely proportional to the square of the distance between their centers.

F = G(m₁ × m₂) / r²

Where:

  • F = Gravitational force (N)
  • G = Universal gravitational constant
  • m₁ = Mass of first object (kg)
  • m₂ = Mass of second object (kg)
  • r = Distance between their centers (m)

Example:

Two objects have masses of 5 kg and 10 kg. Their distance is 2 m. Find the gravitational force.

Given:

  • m₁ = 5 kg
  • m₂ = 10 kg
  • r = 2 m
  • G = 6.67 × 10⁻¹¹ Nm²/kg²
F = G(m₁ × m₂) / r²
F = (6.67 × 10⁻¹¹ × 5 × 10) / 2²
F = 8.34 × 10⁻¹⁰ N

3. Key Points of Newton's Law of Gravitation

  • Every object attracts every other object.
  • Gravitational force is always attractive.
  • Greater masses produce greater gravitational force.
  • Greater distance produces smaller gravitational force.
  • The force acts along the line joining the centers of two objects.
  • The gravitational force follows the inverse-square law.

4. Newton's Law of Gravitation and Newton's Third Law

Newton's third law states that every action has an equal and opposite reaction.

According to the law of gravitation, the Earth attracts the Moon, and the Moon also attracts the Earth.

Both forces have equal magnitude but opposite directions. They act on different objects.

F(Earth on Moon) = -F(Moon on Earth)

This is an example of Newton's third law of motion.

5. Newton's Three Laws of Motion (5 Lines)

  1. First Law: An object remains at rest or in uniform motion unless an external force acts on it.
  2. Second Law: The acceleration of an object depends on the applied force and its mass.
  3. Formula: F = ma, where F is force, m is mass and a is acceleration.
  4. Third Law: Every action has an equal and opposite reaction.
  5. Example: When we push a wall, the wall pushes us back with an equal and opposite force.

6. Gravitational Field

A gravitational field is the region around a massive object in which another object experiences gravitational force.

Example:

The Earth has a gravitational field around it. Any object placed in this field experiences gravitational attraction.

g = F / m
  • g = Gravitational field strength
  • F = Gravitational force
  • m = Mass of the object

SI unit of gravitational field strength is N/kg.

7. Universal Gravitational Constant (G)

The universal gravitational constant is the constant used in Newton's law of gravitation.

G = 6.67 × 10⁻¹¹ Nm²/kg²

Important Points:

  • It is represented by capital G.
  • Its value is the same throughout the universe.
  • It is a scalar constant.
  • Its SI unit is Nm²/kg².

8. Mass of the Earth

The mass of the Earth can be calculated by using the gravitational acceleration at its surface.

At the Earth's surface:

g = GM / R²

Rearranging the formula:

M = gR² / G

Where:

  • M = Mass of Earth
  • g = Acceleration due to gravity (9.8 m/s²)
  • R = Radius of Earth (6.37 × 10⁶ m)
  • G = Universal gravitational constant

Example:

Using the above values:

M = (9.8 × (6.37 × 10⁶)²) / (6.67 × 10⁻¹¹)
M ≈ 5.97 × 10²⁴ kg

Answer: The mass of Earth is approximately 5.97 × 10²⁴ kg.

9. Artificial Satellites

An artificial satellite is a man-made object sent into space to revolve around the Earth or another celestial body.

Uses of Artificial Satellites:

  • Communication
  • Weather forecasting
  • Navigation and GPS
  • Scientific research
  • Earth observation

10. Newton's Law of Gravitation in the Motion of a Satellite

An artificial satellite revolves around the Earth because the Earth attracts it through gravitational force.

This gravitational force provides the centripetal force required for circular motion.

Gravitational Force = Centripetal Force
GMm / r² = mv² / r

After simplifying:

v = √(GM / r)

This formula gives the orbital speed of a satellite in a circular orbit.

11. Motion of an Artificial Satellite Around the Earth

An artificial satellite moves around the Earth in an orbit due to gravitational attraction.

  • Earth's gravitational force acts towards its center.
  • This force provides centripetal force.
  • The satellite has a tangential velocity.
  • Its continuous motion around Earth forms an orbit.

For a circular orbit, the orbital speed is:

v = √(GM / r)

Here, r is the distance from the center of Earth to the satellite.

12. Types of Artificial Satellites

1. Communication Satellites

These satellites are used for television, telephone and internet communication.

2. Weather Satellites

These satellites observe clouds, storms and weather conditions.

3. Navigation Satellites

These satellites help us find locations and directions using systems such as GPS.

4. Scientific Satellites

These satellites collect information for scientific research.

5. Earth Observation Satellites

These satellites take pictures and collect information about the Earth's surface.

13. Mass of a Satellite

The mass of a satellite is the amount of matter contained in it.

When a satellite moves around Earth, its mass remains constant. Its weight depends on the gravitational field at its location.

F = GMm / r²

Rearranging this equation to calculate satellite mass:

m = Fr² / GM

Where:

  • m = Mass of satellite
  • F = Gravitational force
  • M = Mass of Earth
  • r = Distance from Earth's center
  • G = Gravitational constant

14. Weight of a Satellite

Weight is the gravitational force acting on a satellite.

W = mg

For a satellite at distance r from the center of Earth:

W = GMm / r²

As the satellite moves farther from Earth, the gravitational field strength and its weight decrease.

15. Orbital Velocity

Orbital velocity is the speed required by a satellite to remain in its orbit around Earth.

v = √(GM / r)

Where:

  • v = Orbital velocity
  • G = Gravitational constant
  • M = Mass of Earth
  • r = Orbital radius

16. Time Period of a Satellite

The time period is the time taken by a satellite to complete one revolution around Earth.

T = 2πr / v

Using the orbital velocity formula:

T = 2π√(r³ / GM)

The SI unit of time period is second (s).

17. Critical Velocity

Critical velocity is the minimum horizontal speed required for a satellite to move in a stable circular orbit at a particular orbital radius, in the ideal model.

v = √(GM / r)

For a satellite very close to the Earth's surface, ignoring air resistance:

v ≈ 7.9 km/s

This is approximately the first cosmic speed near Earth's surface.

18. Important Formulas

Physical Quantity Formula
Gravitational Force F = Gm₁m₂ / r²
Gravitational Field Strength g = F / m
Weight W = mg
Mass of Earth M = gR² / G
Orbital Velocity v = √(GM / r)
Time Period T = 2π√(r³ / GM)
Gravitational Force on Satellite F = GMm / r²

19. Solved Numerical Example

Question: Calculate the orbital velocity of a satellite if GM = 3.986 × 10¹⁴ m³/s² and its orbital radius is 7.0 × 10⁶ m.

Given:

  • GM = 3.986 × 10¹⁴ m³/s²
  • r = 7.0 × 10⁶ m

Formula:

v = √(GM / r)

Solution:

v = √((3.986 × 10¹⁴) / (7.0 × 10⁶))
v = √(5.694 × 10⁷)
v ≈ 7546 m/s

Answer: Orbital velocity is approximately 7.55 × 10³ m/s.
